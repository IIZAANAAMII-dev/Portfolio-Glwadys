# Verdict

The visual system is considered, but the current experience does not yet feel like a flagship portfolio. It feels like an authored luxury-portfolio concept whose strongest material—Glwadys’s actual work and decisions—is missing. The mobile screenshots show collisions, the contact page obscures key information, and generated still lifes stand in for project evidence.

| Area | Read |
|---|---|
| Concept | “Creative notebook” gives the site a frame, but “stories people remember” and the six-step strategy could belong to many creatives. |
| Composition | The hero has impact. The process and contact compositions lose hierarchy as images and type compete. |
| Typography | Bodoni and the sans-serif create a clear contrast, but Didone plus ivory, oxblood, and tracked micro-labels is familiar luxury-portfolio language. |
| Art direction | Warm color and asymmetry are strengths. The repeated paper edges, folds, notes, stamps, and grain make the notebook metaphor too literal. |
| Originality | The persistent image moving from hero to content to phone is the most authored idea. The portal and floating image collage are less distinctive on their own. |
| Image treatment | The restrained palette is cohesive; the generated, repeated still lifes feel generic and cannot prove client work. |
| Emotional arc | “Finished content → strategy → work → contact” is a sound arc. In the screenshots, the strategy scene is crowded and the ending is obstructed. |
| Mobile authorship | Weak in the supplied states. Floating image strips cover project copy and contact content; the bottom nav crowds the viewport. |
| Authored vs. template-like | The structure has authored intent. The imagery and familiar luxury styling currently make the result read more like a polished demo than a singular personal portfolio. |

## KEEP

- **The dark, warm palette**, with ivory and oxblood as restrained counterparts.
- **A strong, quiet opening** with Glwadys’s name and a clear role statement.
- **The persistent image handoff** from a finished piece of content into the thinking behind it. It directly supports the portfolio’s thesis.
- **The distinct project contexts**: jewellery, community, and retail should each retain their own visual character.
- **The multilingual experience and Paris–Seoul axis**, once the geography is explained clearly and consistently.

## KILL

- **Generated imagery as evidence of client work.** It may serve as a clearly identified concept visual, but it cannot stand in for campaign output.
- **The phone-to-WebGL portal as the centerpiece.** It is an expensive spectacle that says more about the site’s implementation than Glwadys’s skill.
- **The faux social-interface details**—feed chrome, timestamps, carousel labels—when they do not show a real deliverable.
- **The accumulation of notebook props.** Folds, stamps, tape, crop marks, grain, and handwritten notes do not all need to appear in the same visual language.
- **The current master tagline as the main proof of positioning.** Keep the idea only if the work makes it specific; the sentence alone is broad.

## REBUILD

- **Project evidence:** Give each case a real artifact, Glwadys’s role, one meaningful decision, and an honest outcome. The current challenge/direction/outcome copy reads as a framework without enough proof.
- **Mid-scroll hierarchy:** The 05.3 method scene needs one clear focal image, a readable step list, and type that does not sit faintly behind competing content.
- **Contact:** Treat the name, email, availability, and CTA as the page’s primary content. The supplied desktop image visibly places a collage over the email area.
- **Mobile compositions:** Recompose each scene for a narrow viewport. The supplied mobile states show image strips covering copy and the fixed navigation pressing into content.
- **Navigation state:** In the desktop process screenshot, “Accueil” remains highlighted while the act counter shows 05 / 09. Make orientation accurate and remove truncation.
- **The scroll story:** Reduce the number of long pinned passages. Several technically elaborate chapters make every beat feel equally important.

## Top 10 defects, in priority order

1. **Mobile image collisions obscure content.** In the project and contact screenshots, narrow image fragments run over summaries, project details, and contact copy.
2. **The contact email is obscured on desktop.** The central collage crosses the contact information, undermining the page’s main purpose.
3. **Generated images are doing the job of portfolio evidence.** The source identifies a generated photo series; repeated luxury still lifes make the work look interchangeable rather than real.
4. **The case studies lack verifiable proof.** Claims such as a more desirable or recognisable presence need an artifact, outcome, or honest qualification.
5. **The method scene has competing layers.** In the supplied desktop frame, the large faint typography, image cluster, and small copy do not resolve into a clear reading order.
6. **The notebook language is overused.** Repeated physical-page cues turn a useful concept into set dressing.
7. **Too many acts rely on pinned, elaborate motion.** The long sequence of reveals risks making the portfolio feel like a motion demo rather than a clear argument for hiring Glwadys.
8. **Navigation gives conflicting orientation.** The active item and act counter disagree in the process screenshot; the mobile capsule also crowds the viewport edge.
9. **The visual language is fashionable but not yet distinctive.** Oversized Didone, ivory, oxblood, and tracked microtype are polished, but widely used in luxury portfolio design.
10. **The geography lacks context.** The hero says Paris ↔ Seoul while availability is Marseille-based. Clarify whether the axis describes aspiration, experience, or working range.

## One coherent flagship direction: **The Edit**

Make the portfolio about **the decision behind the image**. The signature interaction is one image changing from finished content into the actual choices that shaped it: the crop opens, the planning or copy enters the margins, and the visual becomes evidence of Glwadys’s thinking. That keeps the strongest handoff while dropping the phone and WebGL spectacle.

- **Opening:** One striking, real campaign artifact. Name, role, location, and a specific point of view. No collage around the email-sized screen.
- **Signature passage:** Finished piece → source decision → content plan. Use real campaign material and a small number of annotations.
- **Selected work:** Three distinct case studies. Yuna leads with product and detail; Marseille Girls Club with people and community; Le Comptoir de Mathilde with retail and material. Each uses its own real artifacts and evidence.
- **Close:** A calm contact page with one clear invitation, visible email, availability, and languages.
- **Visual grammar:** Keep the warm palette and expressive display serif. Use the sans-serif for all reading and metadata. Treat a contact sheet as an occasional organizing device, not a reason to make every section look like a physical notebook. Mobile gets a deliberate vertical composition, not a reduced desktop collage.

The emotional arc should be **recognition → reveal → proof → invitation**. The strongest image earns attention; the edit reveals judgment; the cases establish credibility; the final page makes contact easy.

## Measurable acceptance criteria

- At **390 × 844**, **375 × 812**, and **1440 × 900**, at the hero, strategy, project, and contact checkpoints, **no image obscures readable copy, a link, or a control**.
- Mobile has **no horizontal page overflow**. The bottom navigation stays at least **16 px** from viewport edges and does not cover essential content; interactive targets are at least **44 × 44 px**.
- Every case has at least **one traceable real artifact**, Glwadys’s role, one concrete creative or strategic decision, and an outcome. Any generated image is labeled as a concept visual and is never represented as client output.
- In a five-second first-view test, **4 of 5 unfamiliar viewers** can identify her role and the work she offers. The first screen also presents a visible path to selected work or contact.
- The scroll experience contains **one signature handoff**, no more than **three pinned passages**, and fits within **12–15 desktop viewport heights**. The interaction can be reversed through scrolling without a flash or lost content.
- Body copy meets **4.5:1 contrast** and large display text meets **3:1**. Small metadata remains readable on mobile.
- At every scroll position, the active navigation label matches the section in view; no label is truncated in the desktop navigation.
- Contact email and CTA are fully visible and operable without scrolling past the final page’s main composition.
